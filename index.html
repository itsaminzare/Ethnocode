EthnoCode

By UCL Anthropologists

AI-assisted coding for field notes and interviews. Every quote is checked against your original text, so you only cite what was really said.

Start coding How it works

Coding fieldwork takes weeks. EthnoCode gives you a first pass in minutes, and shows exactly where every quote came from.

Paste a field note or upload an interview transcript. Claude proposes codes with definitions and supporting quotes. You review, edit and build them into a codebook you can take into your thesis or paper.

Try it with a sample field note

How it works

The same open and axial coding you already do, with the first draft done for you.

  1. 1

    Add your notes

    Paste text, or upload .txt and .docx files. Notes are saved in your browser as you work.

  2. 2

    Generate codes

    Claude proposes four to seven codes per note, each with a short definition and exact supporting quotes.

  3. 3

    Check the evidence

    Every quote is matched against your text. Coded passages are highlighted, and anything not found word for word is flagged.

  4. 4

    Build the codebook

    Codes from all your notes are grouped into themes. Export the codebook to Word, CSV or Markdown.

Every quote is checked against your note

Language models paraphrase. In research, a paraphrase presented as a quote is a mistake. EthnoCode compares every quote with your original text and tells you which ones you can cite.

Field note: Tuesday market, 14:203 codes

Noticed vendors constantly redistributing produce between each other’s stalls: a crate of tomatoes moved from stall 4 to stall 7 without any visible exchange of money. When I asked about it later, the tomato seller said we’re not really competitors, we’re neighbors first.

A dispute broke out around 15:00 between two fish vendors. Bystanders didn’t intervene directly, but several older vendors positioned themselves nearby, visibly listening, without speaking. The loud vendor immediately lowered his voice and the disagreement ended.

  • Reciprocal stocking we’re not really competitors, we’re neighbors first Verified in your note
  • Silent arbitration several older vendors positioned themselves nearby, visibly listening, without speaking Verified in your note
  • Formal and lived rules vendors share stock instead of competing Not in your note: paraphrased

Matching ignores spacing, capitals and curly quote marks, but never rewording.

Made by anthropologists, for qualitative research

We built EthnoCode because we wanted the speed of AI without giving up the standards our discipline holds us to.

You stay the analyst

Claude drafts. You decide. Every code can be renamed, edited or deleted, and nothing goes into the codebook without you building it.

Traceable by design

Click any quote, in a code or a theme, and EthnoCode takes you to the exact passage in the original note.

Your notes stay with you

Work without an account and notes stay in your browser, or create a free account to save them privately and pick up on any device. Text is sent to Claude only when you choose to generate codes.
